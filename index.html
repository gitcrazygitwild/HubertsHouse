How the three predictions work (Hubert, Mia, Mack)

Hubert’s Purrfect Window is an independent estimate using up to 14 days of logged sleep. It prefers wake windows around the same time of day, uses the median of up to 20 recent windows, and shows a tight window around it: a quarter of the spread of those windows, 5–12 minutes either side. It needs at least three windows across two days. In the evening, when most wake-ups around this time were followed by the night’s sleep, it forecasts bedtime the same way. Overnight, while the sleep timer runs, it forecasts her next wake-up from night stretches that started around the same time of night. Mia’s Catnap Forecast works differently: it compares only the same nap of the day (wake-up → nap 1, nap 1 → nap 2…), counts recent days more, sets aside logging outliers (like a forgotten nap), and follows how her windows grow. With Frankie’s birthdate from Huckleberry, that growth follows her age (fast in the early months, gentler later), and older babies’ history counts for longer (each day’s weight halves every 3 days under 3 months, up to 8 days after a year). It nudges the window when the last nap or last night was shorter or longer than usual, and for an early or late wake-up or a bigger or smaller bottle when her history shows those matter, each by how much is learned from Frankie’s own logs. If she usually falls asleep a steady time after a bottle, Mia leans on today’s bottle once it’s given. After her usual number of naps (or when another nap would run into bedtime), Mia forecasts bedtime instead, blending the usual gap from the last nap, her usual bedtime and her pre-bed bottle, trusting whichever has been steadiest. Nap or night isn’t a fixed hour: Mia learns Frankie’s own bedtimes, so a late catnap before a 10 PM bedtime still counts as a nap. It needs that nap, or bedtime, on at least three days (two with her birthdate). While she naps, Mia also calls the wake-up from how long that nap of the day usually lasts. Mia’s windows are kept tight (5–10 minutes either side). Each time she wakes, all three call whether the next sleep is a nap or the night (Challenge 2 on the Frankie page); at night they make a morning wake-up call (Challenge 3), and each day they bet on her ounces (Challenge 4). Mack’s pick is Huckleberry’s SweetSpot: he was asleep in the laundry basket while the others built their models, so he copies it and takes full credit. It’s Huckleberry’s last stored prediction; opening Huckleberry may be needed to calculate a new one. Hubert and Mia recalculate after every nap, including backfilled ones; SweetSpot is Huckleberry’s latest prediction while Frankie is awake. Naps with all three guesses are scored on the Frankie page, each by the model version that was live at the time. Missing logs can skew it. Follow your baby’s cues; this is a pattern estimate, not medical advice.
